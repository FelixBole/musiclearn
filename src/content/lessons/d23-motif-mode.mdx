A **leitmotif** is a short musical idea tied to a character, place, object or emotion. Wagner built entire operas from them; film and game composers use them to tell stories without words. The craft lies in **changing** the motif as the story changes, while keeping it recognisable.

## What keeps a motif recognisable

Listeners remember two things above all: **rhythm** and **contour** (the up-and-down shape). As long as those survive, you can change almost everything else: key, mode, harmony, orchestration, tempo.

Here's a hero's motif in C major, and the same degrees in other modes:

<Motif notes="G4:q C5:q. D5:8 E5:q D5:q C5:h" tonic="C" show="original,mode,lydian,dorian,phrygian" />

- **Minor**: the hero is in trouble, or grieving.
- **Lydian**: the hero discovers something magical.
- **Dorian**: determination, a noble struggle.
- **Phrygian**: corruption: the villain's version of the hero.

## Reharmonization

Keep the melody exactly the same and change the **chords underneath**. The motif's notes stay, but the meaning shifts:

<Motif notes="G4:q C5:q. D5:8 E5:q D5:q C5:h" tonic="C" show="original" harmony="modal" />

The same melody note can sit on many chords: an E can be the 3rd of C major, the 5th of A minor, the root of E major or the 7th of F major. Each gives a different emotion.
