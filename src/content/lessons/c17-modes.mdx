The **modes** are seven scales that use the white keys, each starting on a different note. The useful way to think of them is not "D to D on the white keys" but as **colours**: major or minor with one note changed.

## From brightest to darkest

Each mode below lowers **one more** note than the one before it:

| Mode | Compared to… | Characteristic note | Characteristic chord | Mood |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lydian** | major | ♯4 | II (major) | wonder, flight, magic |
| **Ionian** | (major) | — | V7 → I | plain major, home |
| **Mixolydian** | major | ♭7 | ♭VII, minor v | heroic, folk, adventure |
| **Dorian** | minor | ♮6 | IV (major) | noble, hopeful minor, Celtic |
| **Aeolian** | (natural minor) | ♭6 | ♭VI, ♭VII | epic, sad, the default "battle" minor |
| **Phrygian** | minor | ♭2 | ♭II | dark, exotic, menacing |
| **Locrian** | minor | ♭2 and ♭5 | diminished i | unstable, horror |

<Callout title="Key signatures for modes">
Modal music is often written with the signature of the related major key (D Dorian with no sharps or flats), as above. You'll also see it written with the tonic's minor or major signature plus accidentals. Both are fine.
</Callout>

<Composer title="Modes as a palette">
Pick a mode for each place or character, the way you'd pick an instrument:

- Elven forest or the sky: **Lydian**
- A heroic journey or a tavern: **Mixolydian** or **Dorian**
- Battle or loss: **Aeolian**
- Desert, cult or villain: **Phrygian**
- Horror or a corrupted area: **Locrian** (in small doses)

Same melody, different mode = the same character in a new situation.
</Composer>
