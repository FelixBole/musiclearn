An **interval** is the distance between two notes. Intervals are the atoms of harmony: chords are stacks of intervals, melodies are chains of them, and voice leading is all about which intervals move where.

- **Melodic** interval: notes one after another
- **Harmonic** interval: notes sounding together

## Counting the size

Count the **letter names** from the lower note to the upper one, **including both**:

C → E: C, D, E = **3rd**. C → G: C D E F G = **5th**. C → C: **octave** (8th).

Accidentals do **not** change the size: C → E♭ and C → E♯ are both 3rds (they change the *quality*, next lesson).

## Reading sizes on the staff instantly

- **2nd**: line → next space (adjacent)
- **3rd**: line → next line, or space → next space
- **5th**: line → line skipping one line (or space → space skipping one)
- **Odd** intervals (3, 5, 7): both notes on the same type (both lines or both spaces)
- **Even** intervals (2, 4, 6, 8): one on a line, one in a space

Octaves and 5ths contain no 3rd, so they are neither major nor minor — just raw, open power. Low brass and strings in octaves, or 'power chords' (root + 5th) under a choir, are the backbone of the epic sound.
