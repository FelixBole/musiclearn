Mode and harmony change a motif's **emotion**. Rhythm and shape transformations change its **energy**, and let you build whole cues out of a single idea.

## The transformations

- **Augmentation** (note values doubled): grandeur, solemnity. The main theme in augmentation, in the brass, is the classic finale or end-credits move.
- **Diminution** (note values halved): urgency, playfulness: the theme becomes a chase.
- **Fragmentation**: only the head of the motif (its first notes), repeated and **sequenced**: perfect for development, transitions and build-ups.
- **Sequence**: the whole motif moved up (or down) a step: momentum.
- **Inversion**: intervals upside down, same rhythm: a "mirror" version, often for an antagonist or a twist.
- **Retrograde**: played backwards: a hidden reference, more for the composer than the listener.

## Building a cue from one motif

A simple and very effective plan:

1. **Intro**: a fragment of the motif, soft, in one instrument.
2. **Build**: the fragment sequenced upward, more instruments each time.
3. **Statement**: the full motif.
4. **Climax**: the motif in augmentation, full brass, while strings play the diminution as an ostinato.
