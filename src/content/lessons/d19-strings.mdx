<Callout title="Sounding vs written pitch">
All ranges in this track are **sounding** pitches (what you hear). The double bass is written an octave higher than it sounds, so its part stays in the bass clef without endless ledger lines. More on transposing instruments in lesson 3 of this unit.

## The section as a choir

Think of the strings as a four-part choir with a bass doubling:

| SATB voice | Strings |
|---|---|
| Soprano | Violins I |
| Alto | Violins II |
| Tenor | Violas |
| Bass | Cellos (+ basses an octave lower) |

Everything you learned about voice leading in Track B applies directly: each section plays a singable line.

<Composer title="Registers are colours">
The same note sounds different on different strings: a violin melody on the G string (around G3–D4) is dark and passionate; the same melody an octave up on the A string is clear and singing. When a theme needs more **intensity**, rather than adding instruments, try moving it to a lower, more strained register of the same instrument. Cellos playing high (around A4) are one of the most emotional sounds in the orchestra.
</Composer>
