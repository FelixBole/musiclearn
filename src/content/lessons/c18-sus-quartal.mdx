Many modern scores avoid the plain major or minor triad entirely. Without a 3rd, a chord is neither happy nor sad: it sounds **open, powerful and unresolved**. Three families of such sounds:

## Suspended chords

Replace the 3rd with the **4th** (sus4) or the **2nd** (sus2).

In classical writing, a suspension **resolves** (4 → 3):

In modern scoring, sus chords often **don't resolve**. They are the colour itself:

## Quartal harmony

Build chords from stacked **4ths** instead of 3rds: D – G – C – F. Quartal voicings are modal, modern and open. They sit naturally in Dorian and Mixolydian, and you can move them in parallel without the "wrong" sound of parallel triads.

<Voicings notes="D3,G3,C4,F4 E3,A3,D4,G4 F3,B3,E4,A4 E3,A3,D4,G4" labels="D quartal|E quartal|F quartal|E quartal" />

Add a 3rd on top of a quartal stack and you get the famous "So What" voicing: three 4ths and a major 3rd (E A D G B).

<Voicings notes="E3,A3,D4,G4,B4" labels="E – A – D – G – B" />

## Clusters

Adjacent seconds sounding together: C – D – E – F. Their effect depends completely on **register** and **dynamics**:

- **High and soft** (violins, celesta): a shimmer, a glow.
- **Middle**: tension, unease.
- **Low**: pure mud. Use only as a deliberate effect (horror, a thunderous impact).

<Composer title="Trailer and hybrid sounds">
- **sus2 / sus4 pads** under a heroic melody: power without committing to major or minor.
- **Power chords** (root + 5th, no 3rd) in distorted synths and low brass hits: the hybrid backbone.
- **Quartal brass**: horns in 4ths for a modern heroic call.
- **High clusters** in divisi violins as a tension bed. Slowly widen them and crescendo into the hit.
