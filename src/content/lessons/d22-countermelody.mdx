A **countermelody** is a second melody that plays against the main one. Done well, it makes a repeated theme sound new and richer. Done badly, the two melodies fight for attention.

## The three rules of thumb

1. **Rhythmic complement**: move when the melody holds, hold when the melody moves.
2. **Contour**: mostly **contrary** or **oblique** motion. Let the countermelody peak when the melody is resting.
3. **Register and colour**: put it in a different octave and section (melody in violins, countermelody in cellos or horns).

Listen to how the lower line moves in quarters exactly where the melody holds a long note, then settles while the melody moves. Neither line hides the other.

## Where countermelodies come from

- **Arpeggiate the harmony** in a different rhythm (the easiest start).
- **Answer the melody**: echo its last motif in the gaps (call and response).
- **Use a related motif**: a second character's theme played against the first: two leitmotifs at once tell a story.
- **Descending scale lines** against a rising melody (or vice versa) almost always work.
