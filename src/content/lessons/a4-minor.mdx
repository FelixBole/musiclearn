## Natural minor

The **natural minor** scale follows: **W – H – W – W – H – W – W**. Compared with its parallel major it has a lowered 3rd, 6th and 7th (♭3, ♭6, ♭7).

<Example notes="A3 B3 C4 D4 E4 F4 G4 A4" label="A natural minor" />

## Relative and parallel

- **Relative minor**: same key signature, tonic a **minor 3rd below** the major tonic. C major ↔ **A minor**, E♭ major ↔ **C minor**, D major ↔ **B minor**.
- **Parallel minor**: same tonic, different signature. C major ↔ **C minor**.

## Harmonic minor — the dramatic one

Natural minor has no leading tone: G → A is a whole step, so the pull to the tonic is weak. **Harmonic minor raises the 7th**: G♯ in A minor. Two big results:

1. The **V chord becomes major** (E–G♯–B in A minor): strong, dramatic cadences.
2. An **augmented 2nd** appears between degrees 6 and 7 (F → G♯): the “exotic”, ancient sound.

<Example notes="A3 B3 C4 D4 E4 F4 G#4 A4" label="A harmonic minor" />

## Melodic minor

To smooth the augmented 2nd in melodies, **melodic minor** raises both 6 and 7 going up (F♯, G♯ in A minor) and traditionally uses natural minor coming down.
