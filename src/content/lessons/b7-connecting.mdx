Here's the practical method to connect any two chords smoothly — the thing that turns “testing notes until it sounds right” into a quick, reliable process.

## The three-step method

1. **Bass first**: place the new bass note (it can leap).
2. **Keep common tones** in the same voice.
3. **Move the other upper voices to the nearest note** of the new chord — usually by step, often in **contrary motion** to the bass.

<Progression romans="I IV I V I" label="Common tones held, short moves" />

Listen to the soprano in that example: it barely moves. That's the goal — let the bass and the harmony do the work, and the upper voices glide.

## No common tones? Contrary motion

IV → V and ii → V-type moves (roots a step apart) share no notes. Move the bass up, and all three upper voices **down** to the nearest chord tones — this automatically avoids parallels.

## Tendency tones

Some notes have a strong pull:

- The **leading tone** (7) rises to the tonic (1) — always in the soprano.
- The **chordal 7th** (e.g. F in G7) **falls by step** (F → E).

<Chorale notes="G2,F3,D4,B4 C3,E3,C4,C5" romans="V7 I" check label="V7 → I: B rises, F falls" />

When the 7th and leading tone resolve correctly, the tritone between them (B–F) collapses inward — the most satisfying resolution in tonal music.
