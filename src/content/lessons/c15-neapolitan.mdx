The **Neapolitan** is a major chord built on the **lowered 2nd degree**: in A minor, B♭ major. It's a pre-dominant (it leads to V), with a uniquely dark, tragic colour.

## Spelling and position

- In A minor: B♭ – D – F. In C (major or minor): D♭ – F – A♭.
- It almost always appears in **first inversion**, written **N⁶**. The bass is then the 4th scale degree (D in A minor), the same bass as iv or ii°6, so it slots into the usual pre-dominant spot.
- **Double the bass** (the 4th degree).

## How ♭2 resolves

The lowered 2nd wants to go **down**:

- Over **V**, it falls a diminished 3rd to the leading tone (B♭ → G♯ in A minor). It's an exotic, sobbing interval.
- Or it falls to the **tonic** first, over a cadential 6/4, and then on to the leading tone:

<Progression romans="i N6 i64 V7 i" tonic="A" minor label="N⁶ → i⁶₄ → V7 → i" />

The Neapolitan's ♭2 is the same note that gives the **Phrygian** mode its menace (unit 17). Use N⁶ at the emotional peak of a sad cue, just before the final cadence. In **root position**, as a full brass chord (♭II → i), it becomes a dark, almost villainous power move, common in fantasy and trailer music.
