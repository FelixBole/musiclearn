A **cadence** is the harmonic punctuation at the end of a phrase. Recognising cadences lets you *feel* the form of a piece — and gives you control over how each phrase of your own music lands.

## Authentic cadences: V → I

**Perfect authentic (PAC)**: V → I, both root position, **soprano ends on the tonic**. A full stop.

<Progression romans="I IV V I" label="PAC" />

**Imperfect authentic (IAC)**: still V → I, but weaker — the soprano ends on the 3rd or 5th, or a chord is inverted. A comma or semicolon.

## Half cadence: … → V

Ending **on** V sounds open, unfinished — a question waiting for an answer. Very common at the end of a first phrase, answered by a PAC in the next.

<Progression romans="I vi ii V" label="Half cadence" />

## Plagal cadence: IV → I

The **“Amen”** cadence. Softer, warm, hymn-like — often *after* a PAC as a coda.

<Progression romans="I V I IV I" label="Plagal (IV → I)" />

## Deceptive cadence: V → vi

The dominant sets up I… and you get **vi** instead. The bass steps up (5 → 6) instead of falling to 1. It extends a phrase and adds emotion.

<Composer title="Cadences in film and game music">
Film composers often *avoid* PACs until the very end — they sound too final mid-scene. The **deceptive cadence to ♭VI** (in minor: V → VI) is a staple of epic music: a huge, bittersweet swell instead of resolution. For looping game music, ending the loop with a **half cadence** makes the restart feel inevitable — the V pulls straight back to the top.
