So far every chord came from the key's own scale. The first and most useful step outside it: **give any chord its own dominant**.

## The idea

In C major, G is the dominant of C. But any major or minor chord can act as a *temporary tonic* for a moment, and get its own dominant in front of it. The dominant of G is D major (D F♯ A), so in C we call it **V/V** ("five of five").

Compare a plain ii → V with V/V → V:

<Progression romans="I ii V7 I" label="I – ii – V7 – I (diatonic)" />

<Progression romans="I V65/V V7 I" label="I – V⁶₅/V – V7 – I (applied dominant)" />

Hear the F♯ in the bass? It's a **borrowed leading tone**: it pulls up into G exactly like B pulls up into C. That extra pull is what makes applied dominants sound purposeful, a little lift toward the next chord.

## How to spell V/x (or V7/x)

1. Find the root of the target chord x.
2. Go up a perfect 5th.
3. Build a **major** triad (add a minor 7th for V7).

| In C major | Target | Applied dominant |
|---|---|---|
| V/ii | D minor | A (A C♯ E) |
| V/iii | E minor | B (B D♯ F♯) |
| V/IV | F major | C (C E G) — add B♭ to make it audible: **V7/IV** |
| V/V | G major | D (D F♯ A) |
| V/vi | A minor | E (E G♯ B) |

<Callout title="Only real chords can be tonicized">
The target must be a major or minor triad. **vii°** can't be a tonic, so there is no "V/vii°".
</Callout>

You can chain them: each chord borrows the dominant of the next.

## Voice-leading rules (the same as for V7 → I)

- The **applied leading tone** (the 3rd of V/x) rises a half step into the target's root.
- The **7th** of V7/x falls by step.
- Don't double the applied leading tone.

The checker and the auto-voicing in this app apply exactly these rules to applied chords.

<Composer title="The lift before the arrival">
V/V before a half cadence is the classic "Hollywood lift": the raised 4th (F♯ in C) brightens the phrase just before it opens up on V. In an orchestral texture, give the borrowed leading tone to a clearly audible line (horns, or the top of the strings) so it really sings into its resolution.
