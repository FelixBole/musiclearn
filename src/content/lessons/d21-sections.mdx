Each section of the orchestra can carry a whole texture on its own, and each has favourite partners when combined.

## Strings: the default

The strings can do almost everything: melody, pads, ostinatos, every dynamic, endless sustain. Many cues are mostly strings with other sections added for colour and climax. Writing for strings means writing in **four or five real parts** (violins I and II, violas, cellos, basses), each a singable line.

## Brass: weight and nobility

- **Horns**: the bridge between brass and strings; warm, heroic; blend with everything.
- **Trumpets**: brilliance and fanfare; use sparingly so they stay special.
- **Trombones and tuba**: weight, chorales, menace.

For a brass chorale, put trombones and tuba low with **open** intervals, horns in the middle, trumpets on top:

<OrchVoicing notes="Bb1,F2,D3,F3,Bb3,D4,F4" sound="brass" labels="B♭ major, brass choir" check />

## Woodwinds: colour and agility

Winds add colour to string lines (a flute doubling the violins an octave up), play solos, and add quick figures (runs, trills, arpeggios). In tutti they're easily covered, so don't give them important material while the brass are loud.

## Combinations that always work

| Combination | Effect |
|---|---|
| Violins + flute (8va) | bright, airy melody |
| Violas + clarinet (unison) | warm, dark melody |
| Cellos + horns (unison) | heroic, rich melody |
| Cellos + bassoon | lyrical bass line with edge |
| Strings pad + horns sustaining the 3rd and 5th | the classic "Hollywood" glow |
| Low strings + trombones + tuba (octaves) | doom, power |
