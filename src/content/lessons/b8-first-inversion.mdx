Root-position chords are strong and grounded. **First-inversion** chords (figured **6**) are lighter — perfect for the middle of a phrase — and they let the bass move **by step** instead of leaping.

## Three workhorses

**I6** — extends the tonic while the bass moves:

<Progression romans="I I6 IV V I" label="I – I6 – IV – V – I" />

**ii6** — *the* classic predominant. Its bass is scale degree 4, which steps up to 5 under V:

<Progression romans="I ii6 V I" label="I – ii6 – V – I" />

**V6** — the leading tone in the bass, rising to the tonic:

<Progression romans="I V6 I IV V I" label="I – V6 – I" />

## Building a bass line

Inversions let you write **melodic bass lines** — a bass that's a tune of its own. Compare the bass lines of these two (press *Bass*):

<Progression romans="I IV V I" label="All root position" />
<Progression romans="I V6 I I6 IV ii6 V I" label="Stepwise bass with inversions" />

Notice V6 always goes to **I**: its bass is the leading tone, which must rise to the tonic.
