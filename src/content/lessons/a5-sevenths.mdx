Add another 3rd on top of a triad and you get a **seventh chord**: root, 3rd, 5th, **7th**.

## The dominant 7th: harmony's engine

**V7 → I** is the strongest motion in tonal music. In G7 (G B D F) → C: B (leading tone) rises to C, F (the 7th) falls to E. The tritone B–F *resolves* inward. You'll study that motion closely in voice leading.

Lush maj7 and m7 chords (often with the 7th in the violas or 2nd violins) give warmth and nostalgia — think of a peaceful village theme. The **diminished 7th** is the classic 'danger' chord; **m(maj7)** — minor with a major 7th — is the villain's chord. In epic tutti passages composers often *leave out* the 7th to keep the sound powerful and open.
