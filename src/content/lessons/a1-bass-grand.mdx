## The bass clef (F clef)

The **bass clef** has two dots that surround the **4th line**, marking it as **F3** — the F below middle C.

<Example clef="bass" notes="G2 B2 D3 F3 A3" label="Lines: G B D F A" />

- **Lines** (bottom → top): **G B D F A** — *Good Boys Do Fine Always*
- **Spaces** (bottom → top): **A C E G** — *All Cows Eat Grass*

<Example clef="bass" notes="A2 C3 E3 G3" label="Spaces: A C E G" />

Notice that bass clef is *not* treble clef shifted down by an octave — the lines have different names. That is why it feels awkward at first. Learn it on its own terms, with its own landmarks:

- **F3** — the line between the dots
- **C3** — the 2nd space
- **middle C (C4)** — one ledger line *above* the staff
- **G2** — the bottom line

<Quiz q="In bass clef, which note is on the top line?" options={["F3", "G3", "A3", "B3"]} answer={2} explain="Lines are G B D F A, so the top line is A3. Above it: B3 in the space, then middle C on the first ledger line." />

## The grand staff

Piano music joins a treble and a bass staff with a brace: the **grand staff**. **Middle C** is the bridge: one ledger line below the treble staff = one ledger line above the bass staff — the *same* pitch.

<Example clef="grand" notes="G2 C3 F3 C4 G4 C5 F5" label="Bass → middle C → treble" />

<Quiz q="On the grand staff, middle C written above the bass staff and middle C written below the treble staff are…" options={["An octave apart", "The same pitch", "A 5th apart"]} answer={1} explain="Same pitch, written from either clef. Pianists choose based on which hand plays it." />

<Composer title="Who reads bass clef?">
Cellos, double basses, bassoons, trombones, tuba, timpani, the left hand of piano and harp. Double basses (and contrabassoon) **sound an octave lower** than written — we will cover this in the instruments unit. When you write your low strings or brass in a DAW, read the bass clef to sanity-check that your “epic low brass” is actually in the instrument's strong register.
</Composer>
