**Four-part writing** — soprano, alto, tenor, bass (SATB) — is the laboratory of harmony. Composers from Bach to Williams learned it first, because every orchestral texture is, at its core, a few voices moving through chords.

## Spacing

Keep **no more than an octave** between soprano–alto and alto–tenor. The gap between tenor and bass can be wider — that mirrors the harmonic series (wide intervals at the bottom, close at the top) and sounds clear.

## Doubling

Four voices, three notes — one must be doubled:

- **Root position**: double the **root** (best), or the 5th.
- **First inversion**: double almost anything — often the soprano note.
- **Second inversion (6/4)**: double the **bass** (the 5th).
- **Never double the leading tone** — it must rise to the tonic, and two copies rising together make parallel octaves.
- The **5th may be omitted**; the **3rd never** (it decides major vs minor).

<Composer title="The harmonic series is your voicing guide">
Wide at the bottom, close at the top: that's how nature stacks overtones, and why it sounds resonant. Low brass in octaves or open 5ths, mid strings in 3rds and 6ths, violins on top. A close-position triad in the low cellos and basses turns to mud — the same spacing rule you just learned, at orchestral scale.
