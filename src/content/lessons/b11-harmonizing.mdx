You have a melody — a leitmotif, a hummed idea — and you need chords. Instead of trying chords at random, use a method.

## Step 1: Plan the cadence

Decide how the phrase ends. For a full stop: the last two melody notes over **V → I** (melody often 2 → 1 or 7 → 1).

## Step 2: List candidate chords for each note

Each scale degree belongs to three diatonic triads:

| Melody note (degree) | Chords containing it |
|---|---|
| 1 | I, IV, vi |
| 2 | ii, V, vii° |
| 3 | I, iii, vi |
| 4 | ii, IV, vii° |
| 5 | I, iii, V |
| 6 | ii, IV, vi |
| 7 | iii, V, vii° |

## Step 3: Choose a functional path

From the candidates, pick a path that follows **T → PD → D → T**, avoiding retrogressions (V → IV). Prefer root motion by 5th, and change chord on strong beats.

## Step 4: Shape the bass

Give the bass its own line — often **contrary motion** to the melody — using inversions where a step is nicer than a leap.

<Composer title="Harmonizing your leitmotifs">
A strong motif can be re-harmonized to change its meaning: the same melody over I – V – I (heroic), over vi – IV – V (bittersweet), or later over borrowed and chromatic chords (Track C) for tragedy or menace. Use this method to find the **plain diatonic** version first — then you'll have something deliberate to transform.
