Three more moves from the film-score toolbox. Each is a small change to familiar harmony, with an outsized emotional effect.

## 1 · The Lydian II: wonder

In a major key, make the ii chord **major**: D major in C. Its F♯ is the raised 4th of the **Lydian** mode.

<Progression romans="I II I II I" tonic="F" label="F – G – F – G: floating" />

There's no dominant pull, just a bright, weightless shimmer. It's the sound of flying, magic and childlike wonder. II often continues to IV (the F♯ → F inflection is part of the charm):

<Progression romans="I II IV I" label="C – D – F – C" />

## 2 · The common-tone diminished 7th

A diminished 7th that shares **one note** with the chord it decorates. Over a C major chord: D♯ – F♯ – A – C, with **C held** (here in the bass). The other three notes slide by half or whole step back into C major.

<Progression romans="I ct°7 I" label="C – ct°7 – C" />

<Progression romans="V ct°7/V V I" label="Decorating V: G – A♯°7/G – G – C" />

<Progression romans="I IV ct°7 I" label="As an approach to I" />

It doesn't function as a dominant; it's pure **embellishment**: a shimmer, a sigh, a moment of nostalgia. It's typical of Golden-Age Hollywood and of musicals.

## 3 · The modern epic loop (minor)

Natural minor has three **major** chords: VI, III and VII. Loop them around the minor tonic:

<Progression romans="i VI III VII i" tonic="A" minor label="Am – F – C – G – Am" />

There's no V and no leading tone, so the loop never "resolves". It just keeps going, which makes it ideal under a driving ostinato. It's the default harmonic engine of modern epic and trailer music.

<Composer title="Putting them to work">
- **Lydian II**: open a magical-forest or flying theme with a I – II vamp in harp, celesta and high strings.
- **ct°7**: a gentle "sigh" in the strings at the end of a phrase, over a held bass. Great for heartfelt moments.
- **Epic loop**: a low string/synth ostinato on the tonic, the chords on top in brass and choir. Change the orchestration every 4 or 8 bars instead of the harmony, and it never gets old.
</Composer>
