If one progression sums up "film score", it's this: a major chord moving to another major chord **a third away**, outside the key. These are **chromatic mediants**.

## Definition

Two chords are chromatic mediants when:

- their roots are a **major or minor 3rd** apart, and
- they have the **same quality** (both major, or both minor), so
- they share exactly **one common tone**, and the other notes move by half or whole steps.

From C major, the four chromatic mediants:

| Chord | Common tone | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **E major** (III) | E | bright, uplifting, a lift |
| **A major** (VI) | E | warm, shimmering, "wonder" |
| **E♭ major** (♭III) | G | bold, heroic, darker |
| **A♭ major** (♭VI) | C | majestic, ominous, huge |

## Why they sound magical

Diatonic progressions are predictable: the ear knows which notes belong. A chromatic mediant brings in notes from **outside the key** (G♯ for E major; A♭ and E♭ for A♭ major), yet it's held to the old chord by a **common tone**. The ear gets surprise and smoothness at once. It doesn't feel like a key change, more like the light changing.

## Voicing them

1. **Hold the common tone** in the same voice (C in C → A♭).
2. Move every other voice by the **smallest step**, often chromatic half steps.
3. Keep chromatic changes in one voice (E → E♭ in the same part). Spreading them across voices creates a **cross-relation**, which the checker flags as a warning.

<Callout title="Chromatic mediants and borrowed chords">
♭III and ♭VI are also borrowed chords (from C minor). III and VI major aren't borrowed from anywhere: they're purely chromatic. Either way, the third relation with one common tone is what gives the sound.
</Callout>

<Composer title="The signature move">
- **Reveal / discovery**: I → III or I → VI, strings sustaining the common tone, horns swelling on the new chord.
- **Power and dread**: I → ♭VI with low brass. Alternate them (I – ♭VI – I – ♭VI) for an ostinato bed under a heroic theme.
- **Minor version**: i → ♭vi (C minor → A♭ minor), even darker, the "something ancient awakens" sound.
