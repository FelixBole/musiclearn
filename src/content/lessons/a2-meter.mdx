## Time signatures

The **time signature** says how beats are grouped in each bar. Top number = how many; bottom number = what kind of note.

- **4/4** — four quarter-note beats (“common time”, C)
- **3/4** — three quarter beats (waltz, or a broad heroic 3)
- **2/4** — two quarter beats (marches)

## Simple vs compound

In **simple meter** each beat divides into **two** (ONE-and TWO-and).
In **compound meter** each beat divides into **three**: **6/8** = 2 beats, each a dotted quarter (**ONE**-two-three **FOUR**-five-six). 9/8 = 3 beats, 12/8 = 4 beats.

<Quiz q="How many beats (pulses) in a bar of 12/8?" options={["12", "6", "4", "3"]} answer={2} explain="Compound meter: divide the top number by 3 → 4 dotted-quarter beats." />

**6/8 vs 3/4** — both hold six eighth notes, but they *feel* different: 6/8 is two big beats (a gallop, a sea shanty, a 'riding into battle' feel); 3/4 is three beats (a waltz).

## Syncopation and tuplets

**Syncopation** shifts accents onto weak beats or off-beats — it creates drive. Hybrid percussion and string ostinatos are full of it (e.g. accents 3+3+2 inside a bar of 4/4 eighths).

A **tuplet** fits notes into an unusual space: a **triplet** plays three notes in the time of two.
