**Harmonic rhythm** is how often the chords change — a separate layer from the melody's rhythm. It's one of the most powerful (and most overlooked) controls you have over a piece's energy.

## Patterns that work

- **Steady** harmonic rhythm (one chord per bar) gives stability.
- **Accelerating** toward a cadence (one per bar, then two per bar in the cadence) pushes into the ending.
- **Changing on strong beats** sounds natural; changing on weak beats sounds syncopated — use it on purpose.
- A **long chord or pedal** can hold while the *melody* moves fast — the contrast creates drama.

<Composer title="Harmonic rhythm as a game-music dial">
In adaptive game music, harmonic rhythm is a dial you can turn with the game state. **Exploration**: one chord every two to four bars, long pads. **Tension**: same chords, but add a pulsing ostinato. **Combat**: faster changes (or a two-chord oscillation every bar) under driving percussion. Because these layers share the same harmony, you can cross-fade between them without breaking the music.
