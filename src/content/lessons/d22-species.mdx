**Counterpoint** is the art of writing two or more *independent* lines that sound good together. Species counterpoint is the traditional training method: a given melody (the **cantus firmus**) in long notes, and a new line written against it under strict rules. You don't need to master it like a student composer, but its rules are exactly what makes a bass line and a melody, or two string lines, feel independent instead of glued together.

## First species: note against note

One note of counterpoint for each note of the cantus. The rules:

1. Use only **consonances**: unison, 3rds, 5th, 6ths, octave. (The 4th counts as a dissonance against the bass.)
2. **No parallel 5ths or octaves**: the lines lose their independence.
3. Prefer **contrary motion**; mix in some parallel 3rds and 6ths.
4. Begin and end on a **perfect consonance** (octave or 5th), with a stepwise approach to the final note.
5. Mostly stepwise melody; balance leaps with a step back.

## Second species: two notes against one

Now the counterpoint moves in half notes. The **strong beat** (with the cantus note) must be consonant. The **weak beat** may be **dissonant**, but only as a **passing tone**: approached and left by step in the same direction.

<Counterpoint upper="A3:h B3:h A3:h B3:h C4:h E4:h D4:w" lower="D3:w F3:w E3:w D3:w" check label="Second species" />

In bar 2, B3 over F3 is a tritone, a dissonance. It's fine because it's a passing tone, walking from A3 up to C4.

<Callout title="Why bother?">
These rules are a distilled version of what makes **any** two lines sound independent: different directions, consonance on the beats, dissonance only in motion. Apply them to your melody and bass line, or to violins against cellos, and the texture instantly sounds more professional.
</Callout>

<Composer title="Bass lines with counterpoint thinking">
Write your bass line **against** the melody, not just on chord roots: contrary motion at cadences, 3rds and 6ths between them, no parallel octaves when both move. Even in an epic tutti, the outer voices (top melody and bass) are a two-voice counterpoint. Get those two right and the middle can be simple pads.
</Composer>
