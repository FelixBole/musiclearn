Keep stacking thirds above a seventh chord and you get **extensions**: the 9th, 11th and 13th. They're the lush colours of film strings, jazz and modern pads. The skill is not finding them but **voicing** them.

## Extensions and add chords

| Name | Notes (on C) | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Cadd9** | C E G D | triad + 9th, no 7th |
| **C6** | C E G A | triad + 6th, no 7th |
| **Cmaj9** | C E G B D | major 7th + 9th |
| **C9** | C E G B♭ D | dominant 7th + 9th |
| **Cm11** | C E♭ G B♭ D F | minor 7th + 9th + 11th |
| **C13** | C E G B♭ D (F) A | dominant 7th + 13th |
| **Cmaj7♯11** | C E G B F♯ | Lydian colour |

The 9th is the 2nd an octave up, the 11th is the 4th, the 13th is the 6th.

## Voicing rules

1. **Drop the 5th** first: it adds nothing to the colour.
2. Keep the **3rd and 7th**: they define the quality.
3. Put the **extension on top** or near the top, where it can be heard as colour.
4. Avoid a natural **11th over a major 3rd** (F against E): it clashes. Use **♯11** (Lydian) on major chords, and the natural 11th on minor chords.
5. With a separate bass, the **root** can be left out of the upper voices.

<Composer title="Lush without mud">
The orchestral rule: **simple low, colourful high**. Basses and cellos play roots and 5ths. The colour tones (7ths, 9ths, ♯11ths) go to violins, flutes, celesta or a synth pad an octave or two up. Extensions in the low register (trombones playing a 9th) turn to mud quickly. Divided strings (divisi) playing an add9 or maj9 voicing in close position are the classic "emotional pad".
