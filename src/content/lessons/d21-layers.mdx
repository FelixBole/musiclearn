Most orchestral textures, from a quiet exploration theme to a boss battle, are built from the same five **jobs**:

| Layer | Job | Typical instruments |
|---|---|---|
| **Melody** | what the listener follows | violins, horns, solo winds |
| **Countermelody** | a second, complementary line | cellos, horns, violas |
| **Pad / harmony** | sustained chords, the colour | strings, low winds, synth pads |
| **Bass** | the foundation | cellos + basses, tuba, low synth |
| **Ostinato / rhythm** | momentum | low strings spiccato, percussion, harp |

You rarely need all five at once. The art is choosing which ones play **now**.

## Keeping layers apart

Three dimensions separate layers so the ear can follow each one:

1. **Register**: the melody above (or below) the pad, never buried inside it.
2. **Rhythm**: long notes against short notes. A busy ostinato under a slow melody works, and two busy layers compete.
3. **Colour**: different sections (brass melody over strings, or strings over a synth bed).

## The ostinato

An **ostinato** is a short repeated figure: often low strings playing 8ths or 16ths on the chord roots. It does two things at once: it states the harmony and drives the rhythm. A good ostinato can carry a two-minute combat loop while melody and pads come and go above it.
